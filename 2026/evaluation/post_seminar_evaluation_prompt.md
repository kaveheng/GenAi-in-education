# Post-seminar AI evaluation prompt

Prompt given, unchanged, to each post-seminar language model (paper 2, *Can AI Grade Dialogic Assessment?*) in every evaluation session. It refers to two attachments: `logs-combined.txt`, the interaction logs of all 36 groups (student data, not published), and `scoring-guide.pdf`, the scoring guide with one page for each AI role. The text below is reproduced verbatim from `prompt_v2.docx`.

---

Description of the idea and procedure

This activity was conducted as part of a seminar in which student groups interacted with a generative AI system after reading and discussing an academic paper within a specific simulation domain. Each group was assigned one of two AI roles: AI as Tutor or AI as Student.

The purpose of the activity was to explore how different AI roles can support students’ engagement, understanding, reflection, and critical evaluation of academic literature. Instead of using AI only as a source of answers, the activity positioned AI as an interactive seminar partner that could either guide students through questioning or learn from students’ explanations.

Before the seminar, students completed a pre-seminar questionnaire about their confidence, preparedness, ability to critically evaluate the paper, ability to support explanations with evidence, and shared understanding within the group. During the seminar, each group interacted with an AI system according to its assigned role. After the seminar, students completed a post-seminar questionnaire about their experience, engagement, satisfaction, understanding, and perceived value of the AI interaction.

The interaction text file (extracted from screenshots) for each group show the actual conversation between the student group and the AI. These interactions (text) will be evaluated independently by another AI system using the same scoring criteria. The goal is to assess the quality of each group’s interaction and performance based on the assigned AI role and the content of the conversation. The file containing all the logs for all the groups are named “logs-combined.txt”

The evaluation should not focus only on whether the AI gave good answers. Instead, it should evaluate how well the student group participated, responded, explained, questioned, reflected, used evidence, and engaged with the AI according to the intended role structure.

For groups where the AI role was Tutor, the AI was expected to guide, challenge, question, and support the students. In this case, the students’ performance should be evaluated based on how well they responded to the tutor’s prompts, demonstrated understanding, used evidence from the paper, reflected critically, and engaged in meaningful dialogue.

For groups where the AI role was Student, the AI was expected to act as a learner that asks questions or requests explanations from the student group. In this case, the students’ performance should be evaluated based on how well they explained concepts to the AI student, clarified misunderstandings, supported their explanations with evidence, and demonstrated teaching-oriented understanding.
